Compare over

Every opposing player who played 60+ minutes counts as a chance against that team; every one who hit DEFCON counts as allowed. Defenders and midfielders are counted separately, since they need different totals (10 and 12). Vs expected compares the hits allowed with what those same players usually manage, so facing elite DEFCON hitters doesn't make a team look leaky. The leak index blends both and is what the picks use: 1.00 is league average.

Where the data comes from

Everything comes from Fantasy Premier League's own public data: the player list, the fixture list, and the points breakdown for every player in every match. A game counts as a DEFCON hit only if FPL actually awarded the 2 points, so these numbers match the FPL site exactly. Double gameweeks count as two separate games.

The 60-minute rule

A game only counts if the player played 60 minutes or more, both for his own hit rate and for the opponent's numbers. A 15-minute cameo isn't a fair chance to reach the threshold. Hits in shorter games still appear in the log, just greyed out.

Which midfielders are included

Only midfielders with at least 2 DEFCON hits this season (in any games) are included. That keeps wingers and attacking midfielders, who almost never reach 12 actions, out of the player lists and out of the chances counted against each team. The list is rebuilt every week, so a midfielder joins as soon as he gets his second hit.

How "allowed" is worked out

  • Chances: opposing defenders (or included midfielders) who played 60+ minutes against the team.
  • Allowed: how many of those hit DEFCON.
  • Expected: the sum of each of those players' own hit rates, leaving that match out. If a team allowed 9 when the players it faced would normally produce 6, it is +3.
  • Leak index: allowed divided by expected, pulled toward 1.00 while the sample is small, so one wild game can't top the table. Home and away versions are pulled toward the team's overall figure the same way.

How picks are scored

  • Hit rate: his hits in 60+ minute games, pulled slightly toward the league average when he has only a few games.
  • Matchup: that rate adjusted by the opponent's leak index against his position, for the right venue, plus the league-wide home/away difference.
  • Minutes: multiplied by how often he has played 60+ minutes for his current club, and by FPL's chance-of-playing figure for the next gameweek when there is a flag.
  • Expected hits is that added up across the chosen gameweeks. Expected points is twice that.

The comparison verdict

The verdict compares expected DEFCON hits over the gameweeks you pick. If two players are within 0.05 expected hits of each other, it calls it too close to call rather than pretending one is better. It only covers DEFCON points: clean sheets, goals, assists and bonus aren't part of it.

Limits

Eight gameweeks is a small sample, especially for midfielders, where each team has only faced a handful of included players. The midfielder leak index leans heavily on the league average until more games are played. Treat it all as a strong lean rather than a certainty. Positions follow FPL's labels, which decide the scoring threshold, even when a player is used elsewhere on the pitch. Rotation, injuries and tactical changes after the last update aren't known until they show up in the data.
